THE STORY BEHIND THE LEGACY

A Life of Purpose

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Growing up in a modest family, he learned the importance of discipline, hard work and responsibility early in life. His curiosity about the world and his interest in science gradually shaped his future.

Kalam studied physics before pursuing aerospace engineering. He later joined India's scientific community and worked with organisations such as ISRO and DRDO. His contributions to India's space and missile programmes earned him the popular title "Missile Man of India."

One of his important contributions was his involvement in India's first satellite launch vehicle programme. As project director of SLV-III, he helped lead the successful launch of the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980. His work became an important milestone in India's space journey.

In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India. Unlike the conventional image of a distant leader, he connected especially well with students and young people. He encouraged them to pursue knowledge, think independently and contribute to society.

After completing his presidential term, he continued teaching, speaking and interacting with students. On 27 July 2015, he passed away while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong. His life remains an example of service, curiosity and dedication to education.
